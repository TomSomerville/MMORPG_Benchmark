# Asset Pipelines 2 — 3D Models, Rigs, Animation and Hair (P10 to P15, P21)

[← Asset Forge](00-asset-forge.md) · Prev: [Images and textures](01-image-pipelines.md) · Next: [Audio](03-audio-pipelines.md)

One pipeline per kind of 3D asset. Shared rules are on the [Asset Forge](00-asset-forge.md) page, including the owner's approval that every pipeline needs before it is made in number ([§7a](00-asset-forge.md#7a-the-owners-approval)). This is the part of the forge most likely to need its fallbacks, so each pipeline's fallback is specified in as much detail as its primary method.

**The rule the 3D pipelines share: a generated mesh is kept.** Where an image-to-3D model makes a prop, a tree, a body or a creature, the game uses that mesh and that texture, reduced, sized and (for anything that moves) fitted to its rig contract. The generated mesh is not used as a target to wrap a template onto. Templates and constructed models are the model-free fallback, and the method for anything that must be dimensionally exact (building kits).

## Shared 3D tools

| Tool | In use, then candidates | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Reference picture | The image model of [P01–P09](01-image-pipelines.md#shared-image-tools) (Z-Image Turbo): one single thing or figure, whole and in view, on a plain flat light grey background, in soft even light with no shadow on the ground | Every image-to-3D job starts from one such picture |
| Image-to-3D model | **In use: Pixal3D with the Trellis 2 shape and texture decoders.** For props and nature it runs as a ComfyUI graph on any role A or B machine: background cut, camera estimated, shape, finer shape, colour, remesh, reduction to the asset's triangle budget, unwrap, bake. For bodies and creatures it runs through AssetFurnace, a local tool that packages the same model. Tried in the character lab and not adopted: Hunyuan3D 2.1 (shape only, no colour; check its licence). Other candidates: TRELLIS.2 run directly (MIT; wants CUDA and is reported to want 24 GB) · TripoSR (MIT; lower detail; small; also runs on CPU) | Bring-up records which run and fit on each machine, with their licences for the owner to confirm |
| Stand-up and sizing | Forge script for generated static models: turn the mesh upright, size it to its contract's box, set its pivot (P10) | Always, for a generated prop |
| **Rig-fit** | Forge tool (`forge rig-fit`) that makes a generated, static, textured figure into a body on its rig contract's skeleton (P13, P14) | Always, for a generated body or creature |
| Clean-up | Weld, drop faces with no area and loose fragments, turn a mesh that came inside out | Always |
| Reduction and UV unwrap | For generated models: the generator's own reduction to the budget (props), or Blender's decimation and a new unwrap with the colour baked across (rig-fit). `xatlas` or Blender Smart UV for constructed hard-surface props | Always |
| Shape pass | Forge script enforcing the proportion and geometry rules in [Art Direction §2](../10-art/10-art-direction.md#2-shape-rules): scale hands, feet, heads and weapon thickness to the stated ratios, bevel hard edges, remove features under 4 cm | Constructed models. A generated mesh keeps the shape it was made with |
| **Repaint pass** | The seven steps in [Art Direction §6](../10-art/10-art-direction.md#the-repaint-pass): bake to the final UVs at the standard pixel density, segment by material, recolour from the palette, repaint the game's lighting from baked occlusion and curvature, apply paint grain. Occlusion is found by casting rays from each face, and edge highlights and creases from the angle between neighbouring faces | Constructed models only. It colours by material, and a generated model has no materials |
| Painted light | For generated static models, in place of the repaint: the generated texture is palette-locked, then made lighter where the surface faces up and darker where it faces down or lies in a hollow. Its colours are kept | Generated props and nature |
| Placing | `forge place <asset id> <file>`: puts a finished file made outside the queue into the game through the same acceptance step as every queued asset ([Placeholders and Swapping §6](04-placeholders-and-swap.md#6-delivery-from-the-forge-to-the-game)), with a record of the method and models that made it | Rig-fitted bodies, creatures and dressed bodies |
| Export | glTF binary (.glb), metres, Y-up, one material where possible, written by the forge's own glTF writer | Always |

**The universal fallback is procedural modelling by the forge's own mesh library.** The forge keeps a library of parametric makers, one per asset family, that build a model from a few numbers and palette colours. They are Python modules that use no Blender API, need no model and no GPU, and run on any machine. A maker exists for every family listed below, so a pipeline can always produce something correct in size, pivot and naming. A fallback model is held to the upper triangle budget only: it may be under the lower bound, and since it is already under half the budget no LOD1 is made for it and the client uses the one mesh at every distance.

## P10 — Static props and weapons (about 320: 145 weapons, 175 props and world objects)

| | |
|---|---|
| Reads | [Characters, Armor & Weapons](../10-art/01-characters-armor-weapons.md) for weapons; [Environments & Props](../10-art/03-environments-props.md) for props; the Named Item Index for unique weapons; each asset's manifest row |
| Steps (props) | 1. **Picture**: the image model paints the one thing alone, whole and standing on the ground, seen from the front and a little above, on the plain grey background. The prompt asks for the proportions of the contract's box ("tall and narrow", "low and wide", "thin from front to back"). 2. **Image-to-3D** at 95% of the top of the asset's triangle budget (props 50–600), with a 1024² texture. 3. **Stand it up.** The mesh arrives leaning by the picture's camera angle. Of the pitches (to 50°) and small rolls (to 12°) tried, the one at which its bounding box is smallest is taken. 4. **Size it** to the contract's box by the rule below, and set the pivot (props: at the base centre). 5. Clean-up. 6. **Texture**: reduce to the contract's size, palette lock toward the zone's ramps, painted light. 7. Export |
| The size rule | The contract's box is what the game places and collides with; a generated prop keeps its own shape inside it. The prop is scaled, all axes alike, to the largest size that fits inside the box, so it touches the box on its tightest side. Each other axis is then drawn out toward the box by **no more than 30%**. A prop that would still fill **less than 45% of any side** is refused and another is made. A flat thing (a flag, a door, a notice board: a box whose thinnest side is under a fifth of its longest) is fitted by its two broad sides and pressed to the box's thickness. Where the contract has no size of its own for the thing, only a class or nothing, the box is marked as a bound (`size_is: bound`): the prop is scaled to fit inside it and not drawn out at all |
| Which props | A prop is sent to the model only if its manifest row says which thing it is. A row that does not (doors and levers, objects named only by a count) stays with the constructed method |
| Weapons | Weapons, attachments and set pieces are made by the constructed method (the fallback below) until an image-to-3D method for them has passed bring-up. Weapons 300–800 triangles; pivot at the grip. The 52 tier weapons are made once each; their colourways are palette remaps of the texture (P06's method) |
| Matched sets | Props that belong together (a camp's tents, crates and racks; a tavern's furniture; a forge's tools) are generated one at a time from the same style fragments and the same zone ramps. No scene-splitting model is used |
| Checks | Triangle count in budget. **Generated prop**: inside its contract's box, reaching it on at least one side, and filling at least 45% of every side (a prop whose box is a bound needs only to be inside it and reach it). **Constructed prop or weapon**: bounding box within 10% of the contract's size on every axis. Pivot and axis correct (a weapon's blade points along +Y from the grip); single closed surface or intentional open edges only; reads as the subject from 3 views (vision checker); held in a hand socket a weapon does not intersect the forearm in the rest pose |
| Fallback | Parametric makers: blade (length, width, curve, guard, pommel), haft weapon (length, head type), bow, shield (shape, boss, rim), staff (length, head ornament), and prop makers for barrel, crate, sack, chest, table, bench, cart, lantern, signpost, tent, fence, well, grave marker, banner, campfire |

## P11 — Modular building kits (20 kits, about 700 pieces)

| | |
|---|---|
| Why different | Kit pieces must snap together on a grid. Generated meshes are never dimensionally exact, so **kits are built procedurally and only their surfaces are generated** |
| Reads | [Environments & Props](../10-art/03-environments-props.md): kits KIT-01 to KIT-20 and their piece lists |
| Steps | 1. For each kit, make its trim-sheet atlas (P05) from the kit's material description: timber and plaster, dwarven cut stone, turf and dry-stone, cavern rock, brick kiln, imperial marble and brass, and so on. 2. A kit maker builds each piece on a 2 m grid (walls 4 m wide by 3 m high, floors 4 × 4 m, door and window openings at fixed positions, roofs at 30° or 45°), mapping faces to the atlas's fixed rows. 3. A second pass adds wear by vertex displacement within 3 cm and bevels on outer edges, so pieces do not look machine-made but still meet at their borders. 4. Unique set pieces (the mill's sails, the Old Forge firebed, the Great Bore, the Hollow Throne) are made with P10 and attached at fixed sockets |
| The shape library | The kit maker has a library of **40 piece shapes**. The manifest names 669 pieces; each is built as the nearest of the 40 shapes by the words of its name, until it is given a shape of its own. The world generator builds buildings from the same 40 shapes, as plain shells (walls with a door, corner posts, a gable roof) painted from the kit's atlas, until buildings are assembled piece by piece |
| Cavern kits | Cavern floor, wall, ceiling and pillar pieces are generated as displaced grids whose **border vertices are fixed to a shared profile**, so any two pieces join without a gap |
| Checks | Every border vertex lies on the grid to 1 mm; pieces of the same kit assemble into the test building without gaps or overlaps (an automated assembly test per kit); triangle budget per whole building; atlas rows used correctly |
| Fallback | Same makers with flat palette colours instead of the generated atlas |

## P12 — Nature: trees, rocks, plants, gathering nodes (about 240)

| | |
|---|---|
| Reads | [Environments & Props](../10-art/03-environments-props.md): 13 nature libraries; 23 gathering nodes |
| Method (constructed; the general method) | Procedural with generated textures. Trees: a branching generator with parameters per species (trunk height and taper, branching angle, canopy shape, leaf card density) and leaf and bark textures from P05. Rocks: displaced icospheres with strata noise and a rock texture. Plants and herbs: crossed cards from generated plant images with the background removed. Gathering nodes: a rock or plant base with a distinct coloured feature (ore vein, bloom) so they read at a distance |
| Method (generated) | For a tree, a rock or a plant that is one thing: 1. The image model pictures it from the side at eye level, whole (a tree from roots to crown), on the plain grey background; "leaf clumps as cards" in a row's description is asked for as large rounded clumps of leaves. 2. Image-to-3D at 95% of the top of the triangle budget. 3. It is left standing as it was made (no stand-up step) and made **as large as fits inside its contract's box**, all axes alike: a generated tree is often as wide as it is tall, and sized by height alone its crown stands far outside the box. 4. Leaves are two-sided, since a canopy is a thin shell seen through its gaps. 5. Texture as for a generated prop. A surface, a cliff face, water or a strip is laid out by the world and is never modelled as one thing |
| Which is used | The constructed method everywhere, except where the generated method has been approved: on 2026-11-24 that is the nature of the two starting zones only. The known weakness of the generated method is a canopy: reduction to a tree's triangle budget can break its thin shell and leave holes |
| Variants | 3–5 seeds per species; autumn, snow and ash variants by palette remap |
| Checks | Triangle budget; trunk base at the origin; leaf cards face outward; a generated tree is inside its box; a node is distinguishable from its non-node neighbour (colour difference above a threshold in a rendered scene) |

Open: P12's generated method is approved for the two starting zones only, by instruction and before the owner had looked at its results; whether it becomes the general method waits on that.

## P13 — Characters and creatures: base models (4 player bodies, 110 customisation pieces, 62 creature rigs' base models, 18 bosses)

| | |
|---|---|
| Reads | [Characters, Armor & Weapons](../10-art/01-characters-armor-weapons.md); [Creatures & NPCs](../10-art/02-creatures-npcs.md); proportion sheets from P01; the rig contract of the body or creature ([Placeholders and Swapping §3.1](04-placeholders-and-swap.md#31-rig-contract-contentrigsrig-idyaml-one-per-rig)) |
| Route | **Picture → image-to-3D → rig-fit → place.** 1. The image model paints the figure: one single stylised painted figure, standing upright and facing the viewer in an **A-pose** (arms straight and held away from the body, legs slightly apart, empty open hands), on the plain grey background; a four-legged creature standing square. The figure is painted as a solid, rounded form, not a flat drawing. 2. Pixal3D (through AssetFurnace) makes a static textured mesh of it. 3. `forge rig-fit <mesh> <rig> <out>` fits that mesh to the rig contract (below, and P14). 4. `forge place` puts the result into the game through the acceptance step |
| Rig-fit, stage one (the mesh) | The generated mesh is **kept**. It is joined and welded into one closed outer skin (its surface thickened by the fewest cells that close it; a hollow model's inner skin is dropped), reduced in Blender to a little under the top of the triangle budget, given a **new UV layout of its own**, and the full model's colour is baked onto a **1024² texture** for the reduced mesh. One mesh, one material. The colour is used as baked: no repaint and no palette lock |
| Rig-fit, stage two (the fit) | The reduced mesh's own joints are found from its outline, skin weights are computed against that skeleton in the mesh's own pose, and every vertex is then carried by its bones from where the mesh's joints are to where the contract's are: a turn, and a stretch along the bone. The result stands in the **contract's rest pose on the contract's skeleton**, bone for bone, at the contract's height. Detail is in P14 |
| What a fitted body is | A single mesh with its own baked texture and its own UVs. Clothing is part of the mesh. So are hair and face on a creature and on a dressed NPC body; a player body has no hair (next row). It therefore does not share the UV layout of the template bodies, and the textures of P06 and painted outfits of P07 do not fit it |
| Player bodies | The 4 bodies are made by this route, one per rig and sex. **Each is painted bald and clean-shaven, in plain underclothes**, so that nothing a player may choose is part of the body. Each replaces a template body, and the template is kept beside it for the painted outfits that still need it ([Placeholders and Swapping §2a](04-placeholders-and-swap.md#2a-fitted-bodies-kept-templates-and-dressed-bodies)) |
| Customisation on a player body | **Separate hair and faces.** Hair styles, facial hair and features (beards, moustaches, braids, sideburn-plaits, rings, earrings) are separate pieces that follow the head, made by [P21](#p21--hair-beards-manes-and-tails). A **face** is a variant of the body's head: a head texture painted onto the head region of the body's texture ([P07](01-image-pipelines.md)), with a small change of shape where it is cheap; freckles and scars are drawn on the face. A **skin tone** is a tint of the skin region of the body's texture, through a skin mask made with the body, and a **hair colour** is a tint ramp on the hair and facial-hair pieces; both leave eyes, lips and clothes alone. The counts are those of the [customisation list](../10-art/01-characters-armor-weapons.md#2-customisation-permutations), and every option goes with every other |
| First results | The four bald bodies and a small first set of pieces are made and shown to the owner. The full customisation set is made only after he approves them ([Asset Forge §7a](00-asset-forge.md)) |
| Dressed NPC bodies | An NPC outfit may be made as a whole body: the route is run once for **one outfit on one body**, from a picture of that person in that outfit, and fitted to that body's rig. The file is stored beside the outfit's texture, named for the outfit and the body, and the client uses it whole |
| Creatures | One fitted mesh per creature rig, made from the picture of one of its varieties. The rig's other varieties are skins: the fitted model's own texture tinted per variety ([P07](01-image-pipelines.md)) |
| Checks | The contract's (every bone and socket, rest pose); one mesh and one material; every vertex weighted, at most 4 bones each, joint indices in range; triangle budget; standing height equals the rig contract's; **deforms cleanly**: posed at a frame of the rig's run clip, its extent stays under 1.8 times its rest extent and every position is finite; mesh structure (UV overlap is not tested: the layout is new); the style gate on the rendered model; reads as the subject from 4 views |
| Tried and not adopted | In a character lab (`forge char-lab`), one picture was run through three local ways of making a rigged body: Pixal3D with SkinTokens for the skeleton, Pixal3D with UniRig, and Hunyuan3D 2.1 with UniRig. A predicted skeleton is not the contract's skeleton, so none replaces rig-fit; a learned skinning may be given to rig-fit as its weights |
| Fallback | A **template mesh** per rig family, made by the model-free maker (biped, dwarf biped, quadruped, bird, spider, serpent, blob, construct, plant), scaled and proportioned by parameters from the description (bulk, limb length, head size) and painted by P07. Template bodies of one family share one UV layout. Every creature can be made this way with no 3D model at all. It is a stand-in: jointed figures made this way were refused by the owner as the game's look |

Open: whether the palette lock, the repaint pass, the triangle budgets and the shared-UV armor system of Art Direction apply to rig-fitted bodies is the owner's decision; today a fitted body keeps its generated colours and has no route for armor regions.

Open: whether dressed bodies are the permanent method for NPC clothing, which needs a manifest row for each, or a measure for the starting zones, is the owner's decision.

## P14 — Rigs and skinning (67 rigs)

The 67 rig contracts are the manifest's 65 rigs (2 player rigs, 62 creature and boss rigs, the horse mount) and two more, the ship and the mine-cart, whose moving parts need bones for their clips.

| | |
|---|---|
| Reads | The rig list in [Creatures & NPCs](../10-art/02-creatures-npcs.md); the rig contracts in `content/rigs/` |
| Method | **The contract's skeleton, always.** A rig's skeleton is data, written before any model exists. A generated model is never given a predicted skeleton; rig-fit brings it to the contract's: 1. **Stand the mesh up** and find which way it faces (an animal may face any way in the file: its left-right mirror plane says which way is across, and the end with more of the animal beyond its legs is the head). The facing may be given by hand where the reading is wrong. 2. **Find the mesh's own joints** with the joint finder of the rig's family (next row). 3. **Skin** in the mesh's own pose: weights computed against the found skeleton, smoothed over the surface, the four strongest bones kept per vertex. 4. **Carry to the rest pose**: each bone's move is a turn from the mesh's bone to the contract's and a stretch along it; every vertex follows its bones by its weights. The stretch of the family's long bones sets the overall size. 5. Write the body with the contract's node tree, the one every clip is bound to |
| Joint finders, by family | **Two-legged** (players, and small folk whose rigs may have a tail and no fingers): from the outline, where the legs part, the armpits, and the centre lines of the arms and legs. **Four-legged**: legs read from the ground up, each foot followed to its body; trunk bones along the spine; a tail followed from its tip. **Spiders** (crude): each foot followed up to its knee; thorax and abdomen. **Floating things** (crude): bound where they are |
| Template skeletons | A template mesh (P13's fallback) comes with the contract's skeleton already in place, skinned by the maker |
| Alternate | A learned skinning model (UniRig, MIT; SkinTokens) may supply the weights for step 3. Its predicted bones are not used |
| Checks | Standard bone names and hierarchy; all sockets present; no vertex without weights; no more than 4 bones per vertex; deforms cleanly in the test pose (P13); volume loss under 15% at joint limits; no self-intersection in the test animation |
| Fallback | A rigid rig: each body part bound to one bone with no blending. It looks stiff but always works |

## P15 — Animation clips (about 850: 204 player and mount, 650 creature)

| | |
|---|---|
| Reads | The clip lists in the two character lists; the clip lengths and contact frames in each rig contract ([Placeholders and Swapping §3.1](04-placeholders-and-swap.md#31-rig-contract-contentrigsrig-idyaml-one-per-rig)) |
| Alternate for bipeds: motion capture from generated video | Generate a short video of the motion with a video model, then recover the 3D human motion from it with **FlowHMR** (code and model published, 2026: physically plausible motion capture from a single video; its authors show exactly this text-to-video-to-motion route). Retarget to the rig as below. FlowHMR needs CUDA; the video model runs on whichever machine has the memory for it. Use this route if no text-to-motion model passes bring-up, or for clips the text-to-motion model does badly |
| Method for bipeds | 1. Text-to-motion model, prompted from the clip's description ("a soldier swings a two-handed sword overhead, steps forward"). Candidates: a current open text-to-motion model that passes bring-up and whose licence allows commercial use. 2. Retarget to the rig's skeleton. 3. Fix foot contacts with inverse kinematics. 4. Trim to the contract's length; place the **contact frame** (the moment a hit lands or a spell releases) at the contract's time. 5. For loops, blend the last 6 frames into the first |
| Method for everything else | Text-to-motion models are trained on human motion, so quadrupeds, birds, spiders, serpents, blobs, constructs and plants use **procedural animation**: a maker per locomotion family builds each clip from parameters (gait pattern, stride, speed, bob, spine wave, wing beat). Attacks, hits and deaths are keyed poses from a per-family pose library with easing |
| Shared clips | A clip is made once per rig, not per creature, and is bound to the contract's skeleton, so it plays unchanged on a template body, a rig-fitted body and a dressed body of that rig. The player clips are made for the human rig and retargeted to the dwarf rig with a height and limb-length correction |
| Checks | Length matches the rig contract's to one frame; loop error (first against last frame) under threshold; feet do not slide more than 3 cm while planted; root motion matches the movement speed for locomotion clips; no joint beyond its limit; no self-intersection |
| Fallback for bipeds | The same procedural method with a biped gait generator and pose library |

## P21 — Hair, beards, manes and tails

A player body (P13) is generated bald and clean-shaven, so that a player's hair and facial hair can be chosen. This pipeline makes them as separate pieces for the customisation list, by the route that makes the body: a picture, an image-to-3D model, then the piece cut from the head it was painted on and fitted to the body's head. A piece made this way is a solid, painted form with no separate locks; it moves because it is attached or skinned to the head and neck bones. A creature, and an NPC drawn as a dressed body, keeps the hair it was generated with as part of its mesh.

| | |
|---|---|
| Makes | The 32 player hair pieces and 22 facial-hair and feature pieces in the [customisation list](../10-art/01-characters-armor-weapons.md#2-customisation-permutations), each for one of the four bald player bodies; and, by the same method, creature manes, crests and tails where a rig has them as separate pieces. Freckles and scars in that list are drawn on the face and are not made here (P13) |
| Runs on | The machine that runs P13's route |
| Primary method | **Picture → image-to-3D → cut to the piece → fit to the head.** The image model paints the body's own bald head wearing the hair style, beard or feature; Pixal3D (through AssetFurnace) makes a textured mesh of that head; the piece is cut from the head and the head is thrown away. The piece is then fitted to the body's head and **attached to the head bone, or skinned to the head and neck bones** where it must move with the jaw or the neck (beards, long hair) |
| Steps | 1. **Paint the reference**: the body's bald head and shoulders wearing the piece, in the style anchors' look, on the plain grey background; front view for hair, three-quarter view for beards. Hair reads as 5–9 large clumps, not strands, in the chunky proportions of [Art Direction](../10-art/10-art-direction.md#2-shape-rules). 2. **Image-to-3D**: a static textured mesh of that head. 3. **Cut to the piece**: the generated head is laid over the body's head, and what lies outside the body's surface is kept as the piece; the rest is dropped. The piece is closed where it was cut. 4. **Fit to the head**: the piece is scaled and placed on the body's scalp, jaw or ear, and any part that passes through the head, neck or shoulders is pushed outward. 5. **Reduce** to the budget (hair 600–1,200 triangles, beards 300–600, rings and earrings under 100), keeping the outline, with a UV layout of its own and its colour baked onto one small texture. 6. **Grey for the ramp**: a hair or facial-hair piece's texture keeps its light and dark and loses its colour, so that the 8 hair-colour ramps tint it and every style comes in every colour from one texture. Rings and earrings keep their own colours. 7. **Attach or skin**: a piece that sits on the skull (a short style, a topknot, an earring) is attached rigidly to the rig's `head` bone; one that hangs below the jaw or the nape (a beard, plaits, long hair) is skinned to the head and neck bones, so it turns with the head and bends at the neck. 8. Export as a piece of its own for that body |
| In the game | A piece adds no bone: it uses the contract's head and neck bones, so every player clip moves it and nothing is simulated |
| Helmets | Each hair style carries a "under helm" variant flag: when a helm is equipped the hair is hidden or swapped for its short variant, as the armor system specifies |
| Checks | Triangle budget; every vertex weighted to the head or neck bones only; the piece does not pass through the head, neck or shoulders in the rest pose or at the head bone's rotation limits, and leaves no gap at the scalp or jaw; outline reads as the described style from front, side and back; a hair piece's texture is grey so the colour ramps apply; the style gate on the piece rendered on its body |
| First results | A small first set of pieces is made for the four bald bodies and shown to the owner with them; the rest are made only after he approves them (P13) |
| Alternates | **HairDeck** (2026; code, models and a demo published): from a single image of a hairstyle it generates a set of stylised hair cards with spring-bone chains that make the hair sway, parented to the rig's `head` bone and simulated by the client. A candidate where a solid piece reads badly |
| Fallback | Procedural hair: a set of 5–9 tapered clumps placed on the scalp from a style template (short, cropped, long, tied, braided, plus beard templates: stubble, short, long, forked, braided), attached or skinned as above. Always works; plainer |
| To verify at bring-up | That a piece cut from a generated head sits cleanly on the body's head, since the two heads are generated apart; that a long piece skinned to the head and neck bones does not pass through the shoulders in the player clips. For the alternate: HairDeck's licence for commercial use, and that it runs within 16 GB |
